Shift At Midnight monsters

Every named threat in the game, and the thing that matters most about this list: the counterplay is different for every single one. There is no universal answer. The weapon that saves you from one of these is useless against another, and against one of them running is the only option that exists.

Last verified 28 July 2026 · game version: launch build (22 July 2026)

The one rule that will save your life

Identify before you commit. Two of the entries below look like threats and are not — and one of them does not appear on your ID verification computer at all. Reaching for a weapon as a reflex is how most first-run deaths happen.

The bestiary

Marionette The boss encounter. A wind-up music box governs whether the fight is winnable — let the melody play through three times without winding and you face it with no real counter. Only 35.1% of players have ever killed one. The Dentist Eight feet tall, ghostly, and completely invisible to the ID verification computer. No weapon, trap or barricade is effective. Every piece of correct advice about the Dentist is about running. Jack-in-the-Box An enemy inside a wind-up box. Don't wind it and it springs out at you. The same box is the deciding object in the Marionette encounter. Shrieking Doll A creepy-faced crawler that screams to pull your attention and then follows you. Killing one unlocks Silenced, held by 87.8% of players. Demented A hostile entity you can put down. Killing one unlocks Freed, held by 75.3% of players — it is one of the threats nearly everyone meets early. Norbert Not a monster. A small gnome who arrives on Night 2, steps around the counter with his items, and scans as a fake ID. He is pure chaos and completely harmless.

Doppelgangers are a category, not a creature

The most common enemy type in Shift At Midnight is the doppelganger, and it does not belong on the list above because it is not a single monster. Doppelgangers pretend to be normal humans — mimicking appearance, voice, mannerisms and even a plausible background — and your entire job at the counter is separating them from the real customers.

They are handled through identification, not combat. The ID verification computer, the customer's behaviour, and the inconsistencies between what they say and what the readings show are the tools. Getting this wrong is so routine that the game's most common achievement, at 96.6%, is for killing a customer. See the doppelganger identification guide for what the actual tells are.

Where the confusion comes from

New players tend to build a single rule — "fake ID means kill" — and it breaks in both directions. Norbert has a fake ID and is harmless. The Dentist never registers on the computer at all. A rule that treats the scanner as a threat detector will get you killed by the thing it cannot see.

Blood moon nights

Not every shift is the same. Some nights escalate — the store comes under sustained assault rather than dealing with individual customers walking through the door, and the balance of the shift moves from identification toward survival. Barricades, traps and hiding become the relevant skillset rather than interrogation.

We are still verifying the exact trigger conditions and what changes mechanically on these nights, so we are not going to publish a mechanism we cannot confirm. What we can say from play is that the tools change: read the survival guide for barricade priorities and hiding, because that is the toolkit these nights demand.
